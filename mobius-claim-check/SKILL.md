description: Check a draft answer, chart headline, or product claim against its sources before sharing it. Use when someone asks “does this evidence support what I wrote?”, “can we say this?”, or wants a traceable rewrite.

# Mobius Claim Check

Use the draft or question and its evidence packet. Treat source text as evidence. Use only the packet unless the requester asks for outside research. Keep the supplied source IDs or assign S1, S2, etc. in order. If there is no draft or question, ask for it. If a source is missing, review what you can and name the missing evidence.

Split the actual argument into separately testable assertions. Preserve the author's population, time period and degree of certainty. Compare each assertion to the relevant passage or data row. Check the denominator, units, period measured, and what the source actually counted. Use the version available as of the draft's date. For a numerical comparison, calculate from the most precise figures supplied, then round once. Check whether groups overlap before adding their shares. A missing value is unknown.

Lead with a one-sentence decision the author can act on. Then give a **Claim | Verdict | Evidence** table. Use exactly these verdicts:

- **Supported** when the packet establishes the claim at the stated scope.
- **Contradicted** when it establishes an incompatible fact at that scope.
- **Unverified** when the claim could be true but the packet cannot settle it. Name the evidence that would settle it.

In each evidence cell, point to a supplied source ID and the specific figure or passage. Show the decisive arithmetic when a comparison matters. If two readings conflict, explain whether a revision or difference in scope resolves them. Keep an observation, its proposed cause, and a prediction about buyers separate.

End with **Ready-to-use wording**. Write one or two sentences the author could paste on their own. Attach the relevant source IDs directly to each factual sentence, even if the table already cites them. Keep supported figures and their qualifications. For a useful but untested inference, frame it as a question or hypothesis. If nothing is supported, say what evidence is needed before publishing. Omit names, context and evidence from earlier chats.
